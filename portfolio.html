Destroy your house to pet your cat!

It's been a long day and you want nothing more than to pet your loving cat...Time to destroy the house to find her! Play a cute game of hide and seek with your best friend and maybe get an insurance payout along the way.

Development Process

During summer of 2024, I and 14 other RIT students had the amazing opportunity to fly out to Kyoto and participate in a game jam in collaboration with the Kyoto Computer Gakuin! Working with the theme “The simple fun of...”, me and my teammates were able to come together and develop “どこネコ破壊”(Doko Neko Hakai). The game has you destroy your house to find your cat and pet her, be careful not to break too much though!

Design Goals

I was in charge of character designs, animations, and the title screen art. I thought a childish drawing style would best suit the tone of the game, and bring out the cuteness of both the cat and the player character. I worked alongside the KCG artist on my team, Mizuki Harada, to create a simple but playful background that would fit the characters as well. Mizuki and I even ended up representing our team in an interview about the experience we had during the game jam, and what we thought of our final product.

The Team!

My team was nothing but kind, patient, and understanding. Though the language barrier did make some aspects a bit of a challenge, we were able to work through them and even laugh about it afterward. I’ve made some unforgettable friends, and I hope to see them again soon!

Remember what they took from you...

A girl version of Omori was planned for the original game but was sadly cut due to time constraints, so I decided to add her back in! This mod replaces sprites, cutscenes, dialogue, animation, and more!

Development Process

Back in 2020, the indie game OMORI was released by OMOCAT and took the world by storm. I started this project out of love for the game and its community, and ended up collaborating with over fourteen talented programmers and artists. I managed the mod from start to finish and guided the team on artwork, animations, programming features, and more. I also hand-made a majority of the assets and even learned how to program to go into the code myself and change in-game events and dialogue. I wanted to catch every detail I could and put out the best mod possible. As of 2025, the mod has over 4000 downloads and has been featured in many fan videos and other mods across the world.

Design Goals

The ultimate goal of the mod was to recreate the game with a girl protagonist as accurately as possible. This meant closely studying the art style of OMORI and hand-drawing/animating many of the assets. Assets that couldn’t be hand-drawn were created via photomanipulation and collage in Photoshop. I taught this method to everyone working on the project to achieve a consistent style that would blend into the base game perfectly. Any asset produced that I felt needed some touchups was either directly edited by me or sent back to the original artist with detailed instructions and examples.

Another Day

Today is another day - but it doesn't have to be. It's important to make time for ourselves sometimes.

Development Process

In fall 2024, I and my friend Puffles entered the 56th Ludum Dare game jam. With only two days to work with, we managed to get 10th place in the Mood category out of more than 1000 entries! Check it out! This jam’s prompt was tiny creatures, so we thought the in-the-browser engine BitsyHD would be the perfect tool to create our game with. The engine only supports 16x16 sprites and a limited 4 color-palette. Keeping these limitations in mind, we decided an atmospheric game would be best, and landed on the idea of a business cat who spends more time focusing on his work than on himself.

Design Goals

I handled the story as well as most of the art and animations. When working with sprites this small and with a limited color palette, it’s important to prioritize silhouette since even 1 pixel can change the look of the character. I looked at several animals and broke them down into their most basic shapes, then experimented with what features I could add while still keeping the readability. As for the story, we thought putting a bunch of happy animals in a village would be too generic of a premise. I suggested we go for a depressing city setting instead, not just for comedic effect but also to convey a feeling of isolation and unease. I’ve always been a fan of simple messages; advice that people can apply to their everyday lives. In today’s age, we prioritize efficiency and work ethic more than anything, even our bodies. We forget to eat, sleep, go for a walk, and pay attention to the things we love. We may never be able to go back to a carefree life, but we can at least do something that makes us smile once in a while.
